Step 2

Understand Your Results

Four numbers matter. Here is what each one means for everyday use.

⬇️

Download Speed

How fast data reaches your devices. It drives streaming, browsing, downloads and video quality. Measured in Mbps.

⬆️

Upload Speed

How fast you send data out. It matters for video calls, cloud backups, posting video and working from home.

⏱️

Ping (Latency)

The delay between your device and the server, in milliseconds. Lower is better, especially for gaming and calls.

📉

Jitter

How much your ping varies from moment to moment. High jitter can cause choppy calls and lag spikes.

Troubleshooting

Why Your Speed May Be Lower Than Expected

Sometimes the plan is fine and something in the home is holding it back. Check these first.

📡

Wi-Fi Distance & Walls

Signal weakens through walls and floors. Test close to the router, then in the room where you have problems.

🔌

Old Router or Modem

Older equipment can cap speeds below what your plan supports. Ask whether your equipment matches your plan.

📱

Too Many Devices

Phones, TVs, consoles and smart devices all share one connection. Streaming and updates can add up fast.

🌆

Peak-Hour Congestion

Evenings are busy on some networks. Test at different times of day to see the pattern.

🔧

Wiring & Plan Limits

Some older lines or lower-tier plans simply top out at modest speeds, no matter what you do at home.

🛡️

VPNs & Background Apps

A VPN, cloud backup or auto-update can use bandwidth. Pause them and test again.

Before You Call

Quick Checklist for an Accurate Test

  • Plug a computer into the router with an Ethernet cable if you can.
  • Pause streaming, downloads and cloud backups.
  • Run the test two or three times and note the average.
  • Test at different times of day, including evening.
  • Write down the plan speed you pay for and your monthly price.
  • Have your service address ready so we can check availability.

Fiber vs. Cable vs. DSL

  • Fiber: often offers upload speeds much closer to download speeds.
  • Cable: widely available with fast downloads, but uploads are usually lower.
  • DSL: uses phone lines. Speeds drop with distance and are often the slowest option.
  • What is available depends on your street and address.

FAQ

Speed Test Questions Answered

What is a good internet speed?

It depends on how many people and devices share the connection. Many households do well with 100-300 Mbps; busy homes with lots of streaming, gaming and remote work often prefer 500 Mbps or more.

Why is my speed lower than the plan I pay for?

Plan speeds are usually listed as 'up to'. Wi-Fi range, older equipment, many connected devices, peak-hour traffic and the line itself can all lower real-world results.

Does this speed test use my data?

A speed test downloads and uploads test data, so it uses some of your bandwidth. If you are on a capped mobile plan, run it on Wi-Fi.

Is my speed test result saved or shared with AIB Communications?

The test runs in a third-party widget. We do not receive your results unless you choose to tell us when you call.

Can AIB Communications fix my slow internet?

We are not your current provider and cannot repair your line or equipment. We can check what other providers and plans are available at your address and compare them to what you pay now.

Will switching providers definitely make my internet faster?

No one can promise that. Availability and real-world speeds vary by address. We help you compare options so you can decide with the facts.

What do I need to have ready when I call?

Your service address, your current provider and monthly price if you know them, and the speeds from your test.

Is calling free?

Yes. 888-566-4246 is a toll-free number and there is no obligation to sign up.
